Kurikaeshi for your class

For whoever teaches the course. Written to be read on a phone, in the order you would do it.

What it is

A flashcard app that decides when to show each word again: sooner while a student is forgetting it, later once it sticks. Five to fifteen minutes a day, offline, on the student's own phone.

Studying needs no account. An account is free and optional; it carries a student's progress to a second phone or a laptop, and it is what makes a class possible.

Two ways to give a class its cards

Send me your vocabulary as a spreadsheet headed word, reading, meaning, lesson; I send back a deck file. Correct a word later and you get a new version; students install it and keep their progress.

A deck shared by a link. On a phone you are signed in on, install the deck, open it, and tap Share deck. Send the link it gives back the way you send anything else. A student who opens it signs in, free, and installs the deck with one tap. A deck that reached your phone as a file shares the same way: sign in, open it, tap Share deck. Correct the deck later, share it again, and the same link opens the new version.

A class. In You, then Classes, create a class with a title and a last day of term. You get a six-character code and a link that carries it. A student entering the code sees the class named, your name, the end date, and one screen listing what is shared, what is not, who sees it, until when, and that this app is not run by their school. Each request lands on your roster for you to approve. Publish a deck to the class and approved students install it there.

What you see once students are studying

Open the class and tap Open the class view. It reads the reviews approved students chose to share, of the decks you published to that class, and answers four questions, live:

  1. Which items the class is failing, ranked by how often a card was wrong on first sight, then how often it keeps being forgotten. This is the list to bring to class.
  2. Whether the class is on pace, per lesson: how many students have seen none, some, most or all of it. A spread, not an average, because an average hides the half of the room that has not started.
  3. Who has stopped, by reviews in the last seven days and the date each student was last active. Dates only.
  4. Whether your correction landed: which version of the deck each student is on.

The view says on its own face what it is: reported by each student's own device, from a record they chose to hand you. Treat it as a submitted worksheet: evidence of what was done, not proof. Nothing here is a grade, and nothing in the app can tell you whether a student was truthful.

It closes by itself on the end date, which nobody can move. A student who leaves closes it at once, including what they shared before. Removing them does the same, and is final: they return only by entering the code and agreeing again.

The file path, without accounts

Unchanged. Ask students for Settings, then Export my review history. The file carries no name, so ask them to put theirs in the file name.

Then, on any device: You, Settings, For whoever teaches the course, Read student exports. Two loads: Load the course deck, the same file you sent the class, read and not installed, then Load export files, all of them. The same four questions; nothing leaves your device.

Privacy, plainly

A student's record is the reviews they did (which card, when, the grade they gave, how long it took), the cards they wrote themselves, and which classes they joined. Not their settings, not the course decks, never a password.

Only that student can read it, until they join a class. Then you read one part: their reviews of the decks you published to that class, once you approve them, and only until they leave, you remove them, or the term ends.

You never see a password or an e-mail address inside the app. Students sign in on the identity service's own page, and your roster shows the name each student typed for you. One tap leaves a class; one more removes everything the app holds for them.

The one link that needs no account

The JAPN 309 kanji deck is public and needs no account:

https://ferrelldevin.github.io/studykit/kurikaeshi/

The first screen offers Study JAPN 309 kanji; one tap installs it. Ask students to add the page to their home screen (Share, then Add to Home Screen on iPhone, the browser's menu on Android) so it opens full screen and offline. A student who wants an account signs in from the browser tab and studies there; the home-screen copy studies offline without one. A course deck never goes on the public site: it reaches your class as a file, or through the class you created.

Built from the course list you supply and a licensed dictionary; every reading is checked against a named source rather than typed from memory. A list that arrives as a picture rather than text is read off the page by hand, and the deck says "unchecked transcription" in its title until you or I have compared it to the original. Sources and licences are shown in the app under About Kurikaeshi.

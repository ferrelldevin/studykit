Writing the Thirty-Two

Kanji Look and Learn 第三課だいさんか ・ 第四課だいよんか · 32 characters · production, by hand

After this you can

Write all 32 characters of 第三課だいさんか and 第四課だいよんか by hand, from an English prompt, with the right number of strokes — and not lose marks on the three pairs that look almost the same.

The two minutes — play me in the car

You already know these 32 characters by sight. The practice sheet asks for something different: it says handwrite Lesson 3 and Lesson 4 and hand it to the instructor, and recognising a shape is not the same skill as producing one from a blank page. So this is the production pass. Three things carry most of it. First, four of these characters are just other characters stacked — a man is a rice field with strength under it, and the word for a gap is a gate with the sun showing through — so that is four shapes you never have to memorise as shapes. Second, six of them are eight strokes or more, and those are the ones that come out crooked when you write them at speed; they need the pen, not the eyes. Third, there are exactly three pairs that get marked wrong by people who know both characters perfectly well, and thirty seconds on those three pairs is worth more than another pass over the easy twenty-nine.

How to run this — three moves

1
Fail it cold first

Blank paper. Work down the English list below and write each character from memory before you look at anything. What you get wrong is your real study list; what you get right is finished. This takes six minutes and saves an hour.

2
Rebuild only what fell

For each miss, find it in the grids below. If it has a build — 男おとこ, 間あいだ — learn the build, not the picture. If it doesn't, write it five times, saying the reading out loud each time.

3
Thirty seconds on the three pairs

Last, before you put the pen down. They are at the bottom of this page. This is the step people skip and it is the one that costs marks.

The rule to carry out of the roomRecognising is not producing. You can read all 32 already — that is why a cold write-out feels unnecessary and is exactly why it works. The sheet grades production, so practise production.

Retrieval 1 — cover the grids and answer out loud.
Which four characters in these two lessons take the most strokes? Name them before you look.

間あいだ 12 · 前まえ 9 · 後うし 9 · 南みなみ 9. Then 東ひがし, 門もん, 学がく and 国くに at 8. Those eight are where handwriting goes wrong under time pressure — give them the extra reps, not the two-stroke 力ちから.

Prompted — the pieces are given, you assemble

Four you don't have to memorise

These are built from characters you already own, which means you can reconstruct them at the desk instead of recalling a picture.

田た+力ちから=男おとこ

A rice field with strength underneath it. Field on top, strength below — get the order right and the character writes itself.

門もん+日ひ=間あいだ

A gate with the sun showing through the gap. 日ひ is from Lesson 2 — you have had it for a week. That is the 12-stroke character reduced to two things you already know.

学がく+生せい=学生がくせい · 先せん+生せい=先生せんせい

Study + life = a student. Ahead + life = the one who got there first, which is the word for teacher. Two words, three characters, and 生せい reads せい in both.

午ご+前まえ=午前ごぜん · 午ご+後ご=午後ごご

Before noon and after noon: a.m. and p.m. Two characters buy you both halves of the clock.

Retrieval 2 — produce it, don't pick it.
Write, by hand, the word for "student" and the word for "teacher." Then say what the shared character means.

学生がくせい and 先生せんせい. The shared character is 生せい — life or birth. A student is study-life; a teacher is ahead-life, the one who arrived first. If you wrote the readings as がくせい and せんせい without hesitating, that pair is done.

The three pairs that cost marks

Every one of these has been marked wrong by somebody who knew both characters perfectly well. Thirty seconds here, every time you study.

母はは 毎まい mother / every. Both are two dots across a bar. 毎まい has a lid on top; 母はは does not. Check the lid before you move on.
力ちから 刀かたな strength / sword. 刀かたな came from Lesson 2, so it can appear on a mixed sheet. The sword leans over; strength stands square.
門もん 間あいだ gate / gap. The same gate both times. 間あいだ has the sun sitting inside it; 門もん is empty. If the gate is empty it is もん.
The trapYou will not confuse these while reading, because the surrounding words tell you which one it must be. You will confuse them while writing, where nothing corrects you. That is exactly the skill the practice sheet grades.
Retrieval 3 — decide, then open.
You have written a gate shape with something inside it. Which character is it, and what does it mean?
門もん — gate
間あいだ — gap, interval (the thing inside is 日ひ, the sun)

An empty gate is 門もん. A gate with the sun in it is 間あいだ, and it means a gap — in space or in time.

Cold — no grids, no hints, this is the quiz's shape

Do this tonight

Blank paper. Write the character for each of these, from English only: outside · behind · gate · gap · noon · south · man · mother · every · country · what · year · past · study · strength · rice field. Then check against the grids above, and write only the misses five times each.

Sixteen prompts, about seven minutes. Whatever falls tonight is what you drill tomorrow — and what falls twice is what you look at on the way into the quiz.

Where this connects to the recognition pass

The recognition pass takes these same 32 characters and teaches you to read them — read the underlined part of a sentence and say it. That is the right order: recognition first, production second, because recognition is what makes production possible. This sheet flips it. The characters have not changed; the direction has. And the pieces you already know are the reason this is short — 日ひ from Lesson 2 is what makes 間あいだ cheap, and 田た and 力ちから from this lesson are what make 男おとこ free.

Three worth asking in class

Q1

On the drill sheet, does stroke order get marked, or only whether the finished character is right? (It changes how your practice time is best spent.)

Q2

後うし reads うし in 後うしろ but ご in 午後ごご. Is there a rule for which reading a character takes in a compound word, or is it word by word at this stage?

Q3

For the Lesson 1–4 quiz, are we asked to write the characters, or only read them? (The answer decides whether the class should be drilling production or recognition in the run-up.)

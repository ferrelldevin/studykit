Kanji Queue for your class

For the instructor. Written to be forwarded to students as-is; the first half is theirs.

For students

What it is. A flashcard app that decides when to show you each word — sooner if you are forgetting it, later if you know it. Five to fifteen minutes a day. It works with no signal and keeps everything on your own phone.

Set it up once.

  1. Open https://ferrelldevin.github.io/studykit/japn309/kanji-queue/ on your phone.
  2. Add it to your home screen — Share → Add to Home Screen on iPhone, the browser menu on Android. It then opens like an app, full screen, offline.
  3. Your instructor will send you a file ending in .json. In the app: Settings → Install from a file, choose it. The course's cards appear on the home screen, grouped by lesson.

Every day. Open it, tap Start today's cards, answer honestly — Again if you did not know it, Good if you did. Stop when it says the queue is clear. It will not give you more; that is on purpose.

When your instructor asks to see your studying. Settings → Export my review history → Copy (or save as a file on Android). Send that to your instructor the way they ask. It contains only what you reviewed and when — no name, no account, nothing else.

Things worth knowing. Set the minutes you have, not a card count; the app works out the rest. Do not reset it to "start over" — that throws away everything you already know. If you switch phones, export first.

For the instructor

Getting the cards in. Send me your vocabulary as a spreadsheet, one row per word, with a header row: word, reading (blank if the word is already kana), meaning, lesson. I turn it into a pack file and send it back; you send that file to the class. When you correct a word or add a lesson, I send a new version of the same file — students install it and keep all their progress. Your name is not in the file.

Seeing what the class did. Ask students to send you their exports. Then, in the app on any device: Settings → For whoever teaches the course → Open the class view → Load export files, choose them all. You get four things:

  1. Which items the class is failing — ranked by how often a word was wrong on first sight, then how often it keeps being forgotten. This is the list to bring to class.
  2. Whether the class is on pace — per lesson, how many students have seen none, some, most, or all of it. Shown as a spread, not an average, because an average hides the half of the room that has not started.
  3. Who has stopped — reviews in the last seven days and the date each export last shows activity. Dates only.
  4. Whether your correction landed — which version of the pack each export came from.

Nothing leaves your device. The app keeps none of it after you leave that screen.

What the numbers can and cannot tell you. An export is a record made by the student's own phone and handed to you by the student — treat it like a submitted worksheet. It shows what was studied, when, and how it went. It does not prove anything about how, and it is not a grade. Nothing in the app detects dishonesty, and I would not build it to claim otherwise.

If a student has a problem. The two that come up: they cleared their browser data (their progress is gone unless they exported — nothing can recover it), or they installed the pack twice under two versions (it updates in place; that is fine). Anything else, send it to me.

Built from the course list you supply and a licensed dictionary; every reading is checked against a named source rather than typed from memory. Sources and licences are shown in the app's footer.
